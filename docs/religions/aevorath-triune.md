# Aevorath the Triune

![Aevorath the Triune](../assets/religions/aevorath/aevorath-triune.webp){ loading=lazy }

> **The Three-Faced God · Keeper of the Turning · Judge of the Veil**

Aevorath the Triune is the god of **life, death, time, and justice**. His faithful teach that these are not separate powers. They are one sacred Turning: life is given, time is spent, death completes the span, and justice weighs what was done between the first breath and the last.

Aevorath is depicted as a single divine body with **three faces and eight arms**. His central face is an ancient white-bearded father, the face of **Time and Judgment**. His radiant feminine face is **Life**, birth, healing, growth, and renewal. His withered, corpse-like face is **Death**, mortality, endings, decay, and the passage of souls.

The church rejects the idea that Life and Death are enemies. A life without an ending becomes corruption; death without renewal becomes sterility; time without justice becomes cruelty; and justice without time becomes blindness.

### Why the Scythe?

The scythe is not merely a symbol of death. It is Aevorath's entire theology made into a tool.

**Life:** the harvest feeds the living.  
**Death:** the stalk must be cut.  
**Time:** the harvest must come in its proper season.  
**Justice:** every field eventually yields what was sown.

The faithful often say:

> **The blade that ends the stalk feeds the child.**

and

> **All reap what they sow.**

## The Hushed Between

Aevorath's priests teach that death is not an instant transition. A soul first enters **the Hushed Between**, an interval outside ordinary mortal time.

There the Three Faces behold the whole measure of a life.

**Life asks:** *What did you protect?*  
**Death asks:** *What did you destroy?*  
**Time asks:** *What did you become?*

Then Aevorath raises the Scales.

The judgment is not a simple tally of virtue and sin. Intent, consequence, mercy, cruelty, sacrifice, responsibility, and opportunity all carry weight. To knowingly pass false judgment is therefore one of the faith's gravest offenses.

Those whose measure is complete pass onward. Those bound by unfinished oaths, stolen lives, soul-binding magic, or terrible injustice can linger in the Between until their measure is resolved.

## Church and Clergy

![Keeper of the Three Faces](../assets/religions/aevorath/keeper-of-three-faces.webp){ loading=lazy }

The organized church is commonly called **the Keepers of the Turning**. It maintains courts, hospices, birthing houses, mortuaries, archives, and shrines aboard Arkflight vessels.

Common clerical titles include:

- **Keeper of the Three Faces** — ordained priest
- **Judge of the Turning** — senior priest, arbiter, or magistrate
- **Hand of the Final Measure** — high priest
- **Voice Between Hours** — supreme hierophant or regional head of the faith
- **Black Harvester** — specialist who hunts undead, soul thieves, and those who profane rightful death
- **Ivory Keeper** — healer, midwife, hospice priest, and guardian of children
- **Chronologer** — keeper of records, sacred calendars, ship-time, and disputed chronology

Clergy commonly wear layered **black and ivory robes joined by antique-gold trim**, symbolizing death and life bound together by time and judgment.

## Temples

![Temple of Aevorath](../assets/religions/aevorath/temple-of-aevorath.webp){ loading=lazy }

Aevorath's larger temples are traditionally divided into three connected sanctuaries.

### Hall of First Breath
A bright chamber devoted to birth, healing, marriage, adoption, growth, and renewal.

### Hall of Measured Hours
The central sanctuary. Great clocks, astrolabes, archives, courtrooms, oath-stones, and the sacred Scales are kept here.

### Hall of Final Silence
A dark and quiet chamber for funerals, preparation of the dead, remembrance, and the recording of names.

Worshipers traditionally enter through First Breath, pass through Measured Hours, and leave through Final Silence—a ritual reminder that everyone walks the same Turning.

## Holy Days

### First Furrow
**Observed:** first planting of the local growing season

The faithful bless seed, soil, expecting parents, new apprentices, and newly commissioned vessels. A single sheaf from the previous harvest is burned so that the old season symbolically feeds the new.

### The Golden Reaping
**Observed:** principal harvest festival

Aevorath's greatest public feast. The first stalk is cut with a consecrated scythe and the first portion of the harvest is given to children, the poor, and travelers. Courts traditionally suspend minor proceedings until the feast is over.

### The Night of Empty Hours
**Observed:** longest night or locally determined year-turn

Clocks are stopped for one hour. No contracts are signed and no formal judgment is pronounced. Families remember the dead, settle old grievances when possible, and sit in silence before restarting the clocks together.

Among Arkflight crews, the ritual is observed according to ship-time even when no natural night exists.

### Day of the Scales
**Observed:** spring or civic equinox

Judges renew their oaths publicly. Debts, disputed inheritances, broken contracts, and unresolved grievances are traditionally brought before arbiters. Knowingly lying during a Day of the Scales hearing is considered a particularly serious sacrilege.

---

## Arkflight Practice

Aevorath has a strong following among voidfarers because Arkflight travel places mortals constantly between survival, death, uncertain time, and command judgment.

Shipboard shrines commonly contain a small hourglass fixed into a gimbaled brass frame so that it remains upright despite the ship's orientation.

A captain may ask a Keeper of the Turning to witness:

- command succession
- last wills
- burial without recovery of the body
- declarations of death after a crew member is lost to the Void
- emergency judgment of mutiny or murder
- disputes over distorted or missing time
- resurrection after a questionable death

The traditional voidfarer's prayer is:

> **Three Faces witness me. Life behind me, Death before me, Time beneath every step. May my measure be just.**

## Priest of the Triune Doctrine

The **Priest of the Triune** is an uncommon Cleric doctrine built on the same proficiency progression as the Cloistered Cleric. Its defining feature is **Turn the Face**.

### Turn the Face ◆
**Cleric, Concentrate, Divine**

Choose **Life, Death, or Time**. You remain aligned with that Face until you use Turn the Face again. You can have only one Face active at a time.

Changing Faces never changes your prepared spells, divine font, spell slots, sanctification, or spell DC.

#### Face of Life
Once per round, when a divine spell you cast restores Hit Points to another creature, you can grant that creature temporary Hit Points equal to the spell's rank until the beginning of your next turn.

#### Face of Death
Once per round, when one of your divine spells deals vitality, void, or spirit damage, you can deal additional spirit damage to one creature damaged by the spell equal to the spell's rank. This doesn't apply to persistent damage.

#### Face of Time
Once per round, after you cast a spell of rank 1 or higher, choose yourself or one creature affected by the spell. That creature gains a +5-foot status bonus to its Speeds until the beginning of your next turn.

### Doctrine Advancement

After its unique 1st-level feature, the doctrine follows the caster-focused Cleric advancement pattern:

- **3rd:** expert Fortitude
- **7th:** expert divine spell attacks and spell DC
- **11th:** expert favored weapon, simple weapons, and unarmed attacks; favored-weapon critical specialization
- **15th:** master divine spell attacks and spell DC
- **19th:** legendary divine spell attacks and spell DC

## Specialty Spells

These uncommon divine spells are associated with Aevorath's church.

### Measure the Hour — Rank 1
*“Every life keeps a clock, though few can hear it.”*

You read the rhythm of a creature's living span, learning whether it is alive, dying, undead, or affected by magic that unnaturally alters its age or lifespan.

### Reap and Renew — Rank 2
*“Nothing ends without feeding what follows.”*

You invoke the harvest principle: one creature suffers measured spirit harm, while its defeat can grant a nearby ally temporary protection. The spell never creates life from nothing; it symbolizes the Turning from ending into continuance.

### Stay the Turning — Rank 3
*“Not yet. Your measure is unfinished.”*

A brief divine interruption can delay the worsening of the dying condition. It cannot stop effects that kill without using the dying rules.

### The Scales Remember — Rank 4
*“The deed is done. The weight remains.”*

You mark a creature whose harmful act you witnessed. If it repeats that harm against the judged victim, divine consequence answers with spirit damage.

### Harvest of Years — Rank 5
*“One moment slows; another finds its stride.”*

You distort relative time, hindering one creature while granting another measured mobility. The spell never permanently steals lifespan.

### Walk the Hushed Between — Rank 6
*“For one measured breath, see as the dead see.”*

Your senses cross the boundary of the Hushed Between, revealing nearby spirits, haunts, incorporeal undead, and magic that binds or manipulates souls.

### The Appointed Hour — Rank 7
*“Death may wait when the hour is false.”*

You shield a creature from premature death, granting substantial protection against death effects and void harm.

### Judgment Without Falsehood — Rank 8
*“Before the Scales, deception has weight.”*

You establish a sacred judicial space that makes deliberate deception difficult and aids attempts to counteract magic concealing testimony or memory.

### Final Harvest — Rank 9
*“What has escaped its ending must finally be reaped.”*

A sweeping manifestation of Aevorath's scythe deals powerful spirit harm, with particular force against undead and beings unnaturally sustained beyond their proper end.

### The Turning Continues — Rank 10
*“The wheel does not stop here.”*

A supreme miracle of preservation and renewal that can prevent catastrophic loss among several allies or restore the very recently fallen without granting permanent immortality.

## Sacred Magic Items

### Scythe of the Last Harvest
A black-steel and ivory-gold sacred scythe. Versions of the weapon are traditionally built using normal PF2e fundamental and property runes, with higher forms granting measured spirit effects against undead or abilities tied to dying and proper passage.

### Scales of the Three Faces
Folding gold-and-obsidian scales used by Judges of the Turning. They assist legitimate judgment, Sense Motive, and formal investigation but do not automatically identify lies.

### Hourglass of the Borrowed Moment
A brass and black-glass hourglass filled with silver sand. Its magic manipulates movement and timing rather than granting unrestricted extra actions.

### Veilkeeper's Censer
A funeral censer whose smoke assists rites involving haunts, possession, incorporeal spirits, undead, and soul-binding magic.

### Vestments of the Three Faces
Sacred vestments exist in three principal forms: **Ivory of Life**, **Grey-Gold of Time**, and **Black of Death**. High-ranking vestments can allow their wearer to choose a protective aspect during daily preparations.

### Judgment Seal
A heavy gold-and-obsidian holy symbol carried by church magistrates. It reinforces legitimate authority during oaths and formal hearings without functioning as automatic truth detection.

### Reliquary of the Hushed Between
A small shrine designed to preserve a temporary spiritual imprint when a body cannot be recovered—especially valued aboard Arkflight vessels.

### Seed of the Final Harvest
A consecrated golden grain sealed in crystal. Used in funerary and resurrection rites, it symbolizes **death → seed → life**.

## Divine Intercession

Aevorath rarely intervenes without cause. His blessings and curses often manifest through clocks, falling grain, impossible shadows, sudden stillness, or scales that move without being touched.

**Minor Boon:** A narrowly failed test may become success as Aevorath grants the single moment in which the right choice was made.

**Moderate Boon:** A creature whose hour is premature can be held at the edge of death once before the blessing must renew.

**Major Boon:** Death itself can be briefly suspended, granting allies a final chance to remove its cause.

**Minor Curse:** False judgment follows the offender; their authority and credibility erode until the wrong is corrected.

**Moderate Curse:** Time becomes unreliable around the offender, producing hesitation and lost moments.

**Major Curse:** Aevorath rejects attempts to escape rightful mortality; magic that unnaturally extends life becomes unreliable and return from death becomes more difficult.
